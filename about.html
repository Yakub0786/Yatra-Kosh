About

A travel agency built to be studied

Yatra Kosh plans package tours and lists hotels across India — and, at the same time, is a research instrument for a Behavioural Data Analytics and UX course.

Why it exists

Most people can describe what a good travel site feels like. Very few can prove it. This project sets out to do the second thing: build a real booking and enquiry flow, watch how people move through it, and change the design based on what the numbers say rather than what we assume.

Everything on the site is functional. The destinations are real places, the package itineraries are realistic, and both the hotel-booking and trip-enquiry flows run end to end. The properties and advisors are invented, and no money changes hands at any point.

What gets recorded

With your permission, the site logs which pages you open, what you click, how far you scroll, what you search for, how you move through forms, and what kind of device and browser you are on. It also records an approximate city from your IP address.

It does not record your name, your email, your password, or anything you type into a booking or enquiry form. Those stay in your browser.

You can decline tracking on your first visit and every feature still works. If you change your mind, clearing this site's storage resets the choice.

How the site is judged

Four UX principles, and where you can see them

Value

Honest write-ups, no booking fee, and named inclusions on every package instead of vague "sightseeing as per itinerary" lines.

Usability

One search box across hotels, destinations and packages; inline form errors that say what to fix; a live price total while you configure a stay or trip.

Adoptability

You can browse, compare, and reach both the booking form and the enquiry form without an account. Registration asks for six fields, one optional.

Desirability

Illustrated posters, ticket-shaped stay cards, a day-by-day itinerary timeline for every package, and a boarding-pass confirmation that feels like a real trip.
